Portfolio Regime vs Market Regime
Market Regime reflects broad market conditions (volatility levels, systemic stress) and is the same for all investors. Portfolio Regime depends on YOUR specific holdings - their correlations, concentration, and composition. A concentrated portfolio may be in "stress" even when markets are calm.

Regime Analysis

Market regimes are classified based on volatility levels and correlation behavior. Regime shifts often occur suddenly and without warning.

Market Regime Global / Illustrative

Market regime reflects broad market conditions that affect all investors equally. These statistics are based on historical patterns.

Normal

Volatility <75th percentile. Correlations stable. ~72% of history.

Transition

Volatility 75th-95th percentile. Early warning. ~21% of history.

Stress

Volatility >95th percentile. Diversification failing. ~7% of history.

Why This Matters
Portfolio models typically use normal correlations to estimate risk. But during crashes, correlations spike—often dramatically. A portfolio that appears diversified under normal conditions may behave like a concentrated bet during stress.

How Mechanisms Interact

These mechanisms don't occur in isolation. A liquidity crisis triggers forced selling, which spikes volatility, which causes correlations to converge as everyone rushes for the exit simultaneously.

Cascade Pattern
  • Initial shock triggers margin calls
  • Forced selling increases volatility
  • Volatility spike raises correlation
  • Diversification fails at the worst moment
What Typically Survives
  • Cash and short-term T-bills
  • Explicit hedges (puts, if bought early)
  • Truly uncorrelated strategies (rare)
  • Nothing is guaranteed

Analytical Note
These mechanisms explain HOW portfolios break down, not WHEN they will break down. Understanding the mechanisms helps you recognize early warning signs and avoid false confidence in diversification.

Methodology

How this analysis works, what it measures, and what it does not claim.

This site demonstrates how standard portfolio assumptions break down during market stress. It shows that correlations between assets spike during crashes—exactly when diversification is supposed to protect you. Using historical return data, the tool detects market regimes, compares normal correlations to tail correlations, and illustrates how portfolios behave under hypothetical stress scenarios. It does not provide investment advice, trading recommendations, or forecasts.
Calculations
1

Data Collection

Daily adjusted close prices are fetched from Yahoo Finance for the specified assets and time period. Returns are calculated as log returns.

2

Regime Detection

Rolling 21-day volatility is calculated and annualized. Regimes are classified based on volatility percentiles: Normal (<75th), Transition (75th-95th), Stress (>95th or high vol + correlation spike).

3

Normal Correlation

Standard Pearson correlation matrix calculated across all observations in the sample period.

4

Tail Correlation

Correlation matrix calculated using only observations where the first asset (market proxy) experienced returns in the bottom 5th percentile.

5

Diversification Ratio

Ratio of weighted average individual volatilities to portfolio volatility. Values above 1.0 indicate diversification benefit; approaching 1.0 indicates diversification failure.

What This Analysis Does
  • Visualizes regime changes over time
  • Compares normal vs. tail correlations
  • Shows how correlations spike during stress
  • Illustrates diversification breakdown
  • Provides historical context for stress events
What This Analysis Does NOT Do
  • Predict future market movements
  • Recommend specific investments
  • Provide trading signals
  • Forecast regime changes
  • Constitute financial advice
Limitations
  • Simple correlation: Uses Pearson correlation, not copula-based tail dependence measures
  • Threshold-based regimes: Uses fixed percentile thresholds, not statistical regime-switching models
  • Historical data only: Past patterns may not repeat; structural breaks are possible
  • No transaction costs: Does not model real-world frictions
  • Linear assumptions: Stress scenarios assume linear transmission of shocks
Purpose
This is a research and teaching tool designed to make one counterintuitive point visible: the assumptions embedded in most portfolio models fail precisely when they matter most. It is intended for educational purposes only.
